## university_guidance
url: https://www.nyu.edu/faculty/teaching-and-learning-resources/teaching-with-generative-tools/frequently-asked-questions.html
source_date: 2026 "Last Updated Monday, October 5, 2026"
> The student must abide by any requirements or limitations the instructor may have in each course.

> AI can be used in a wide variety of ways. Because of its flexibility, there are few standard approaches to its use beyond an institution-wide restriction on taking credit for AI output without acknowledging its use. Most policies will be set by the schools, departments, or by individual faculty members.

> Academic integrity violations can only be defined in a particular context. Using AI to create work for a course violates the rules when a course forbids it, but does not when a course allows it.

> If faculty suspect that a student may have used AI in a way that was not permitted, they should treat it as contract cheating.

## intro_course
course: CSCI-UA 102 Data Structures (sec 10, Klukowska), Fall 2026
url: https://cims.nyu.edu/~joannakl/cs102/syllabus.html
source_date: undated
> In this class, we will not ban these tools; instead, we will attempt to show you how to use them to your benefit rather than to your detriment.

> Using AI to do all of your coding work for you will be a detriment to your learning.

> AI as a Partner in Peer Programming: Sometimes AI can be used effectively to speed up coding the ideas that you have. Come up with a detailed step by step algorithm and ask it to generate code that follows those steps exactly.

> Keep in mind that exams and other assessments in this class will not be given with access to AI tools, so you need to be able to write complete programs, implement parts of given programs, answer questions and suggest solutions to problems on your own without the help of any tools.
