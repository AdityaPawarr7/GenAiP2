## university_guidance
url: https://citl.illinois.edu/generative-artificial-intelligence-gen-ai/teaching-implications
source_date: undated
> Faculty should clearly define the boundaries for using AI in student work to maintain academic integrity and discourage plagiarism. Students should be educated about the ethical use of AI tools and reminded that text and ideas generated by AI must be cited to avoid plagiarism. Your syllabus policies should address the importance of academic integrity and the use of AI.

> The knee-jerk response is to add a blanket AI policy to your syllabus, but this is not advised. Instead look for interesting ways to introduce AI into the course.

> Additionally, students have a right to use these tools to enhance their own learning. By being unnecessarily punitive in your policies you risk negatively impacting your student's academic growth and understanding of these tools.

url: https://genai.illinois.edu/best-practices-teaching-and-learning/
source_date: undated
> Instructors and learners should use AI in ethical ways. Transparency regarding the role of AI in learning activities and academic work supports academic integrity. Learners are responsible for doing the work to learn.

## intro_course

source_date: 2026 "CS 124: Fall 2026 Syllabus"
> All homework and quiz code you submit must be written by you. Not by another student, not from the internet, and not by an AI assistant.

> To be clear about where the line falls: asking questions is fine, getting answers is not.

> On the project, an AI coding agent will write most of your code, and we expect it to. Learning to direct one well is the point of that half of the course, so the rules that govern the homework do not transfer, and applying them here would be nonsense.

course: CS 128 Introduction to Computer Science II, Fall 2026
url: https://cs128.org/syllabus
source_date: 2026 "Syllabus Fall 2026"
> Using AI in this course is optional. You can complete every assignment and earn full marks without ever using an AI tool. If you do use one, the rules in this policy apply.

> Stated as a rule of use, AI may support your process, e.g., understanding the problem, learning relevant concepts, developing a strategy, debugging your attempts, and verifying correctness, but it may not produce your product, i.e., the code you submit, which you must be able to reproduce independently.

> For every graded lesson, recitation activity, and machine problem on which you use AI assistance, however minimal or extensive, you must submit the AI Usage Reflection Form together with the complete transcript of your AI interactions.
