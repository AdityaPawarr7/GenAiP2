## university_guidance
url: https://ctlm.wisc.edu/self-serve-resources/generativeai/uw-madison-guiding-principles-for-generative-ai-in-teaching
source_date: undated
> You, as the instructor, might have a policy for the entire course, or you might have specific instructions for individual assignments and assessments. Recognize that students might receive different guidelines from other instructors.

> If you plan to discourage or prohibit the use of generative AI in your course, it is particularly important that you explain why to students in the context of learning outcomes for the course.

> Avoid generative AI detection tools; they are imperfect at best, carry the risk of false positives, have been shown to be biased against non-native English speakers, and will not prevent students from using these tools. UW–Madison has decided not to offer an enterprise tool for AI detection due to these issues.

url: https://conduct.students.wisc.edu/faculty-staff-resources/artificial-intelligence/
source_date: undated
> Students are responsible for knowing their instructor’s expectations when it comes to using AI tools.

> If an instructor does not allow the use of AI tools and they suspect a student has used them, they will invite the student to meet with them about the possible violation of UWS 14.03(1)(b) Use of Unauthorized Materials.
