## university_guidance
url: https://senate.uci.edu/committees/councils/council-on-educational-policy-cep/council-on-educational-policy-manual/guidelines-for-course-syllabi/
source_date: undated
> All course syllabi must include a policy on student use of generative AI in the course. There is no general campus policy on student use of generative AI, and the content of the policy is at the discretion of the instructor.

> Your syllabus should explain how and why using generative AI is or is not acceptable for the course, for each assignment, and/or for specific tasks within assignments. There may be cases within the course where AI is a helpful learning tool and others where it is strictly forbidden.

url: https://dtei.uci.edu/generative-ai/syllabus/
source_date: 2026 "established the following policy (effective February 12, 2026)"
> Recent guidance from the UC Irvine Academic Senate, through the Council on Educational Policy, now requires that all undergraduate course syllabi include a clear statement addressing the use of generative AI tools.

> We want to emphasize that the absence of a single campuswide policy is intentional.

url: https://conduct.uci.edu/academic-integrity/
source_date: undated
> Examples of Plagiarism include but are not limited to: Using work created by Generative AI tools and failing to include appropriate citation.

> Representing artificially-produced text as if it were your own language in writing.

> Plagiarism occurs when you submit an assignment, a part of an assignment, or a creative work as your own when it was originally created by someone else or by generative AI.

## intro_course
course: I&C SCI 33 Intermediate Programming (Alex Thornton), Fall 2026
url: https://ics.uci.edu/~thornton/ics33/CourseReference.html
source_date: 2026 "ICS 33 Fall 2026, Course Reference"
> Please note that the use of generative AI tools is prohibited in this course. The primary educational goal is developing one's own programming skills, which can't be done if someone else is doing all of the heavy lifting.

> Note, also, that this disallows the use of automatic systems, such as generative AI systems like ChatGPT, to produce any part of your solution, because that, too, would not be code written solely by you

> If you ask a generative AI system to write a solution to some or all of an assignment, you've crossed the line.
